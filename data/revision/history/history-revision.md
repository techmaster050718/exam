## 1. Prehistoric Period

| Phase | Period | Features |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Palaeolithic | 500,000 – 10,000 BCE | Stone tools (hand axes, cleavers); hunters; Bhimbetka caves (MP) |
| Mesolithic | 10,000 – 8,000 BCE | Microlithic tools; semi-nomadic |
| Neolithic | 8,000 – 4,000 BCE | Agriculture begins; polished tools; Mehrgarh (oldest farming site, Baluchistan) |
| Chalcolithic | 4,000 – 1,500 BCE | Copper + stone tools; Jorwe, Kayatha, Malwa cultures |

> **⚠️ Trap:** Bhimbetka → Palaeolithic rock paintings, NOT Neolithic.

## 2. Indus Valley Civilisation (IVC)

### 2.1 Timeline
| Phase | Dates |
|-------|-------|
| Early (Pre-urban) | ~3300–2600 BCE |
| Mature (Urban) | ~2600–1900 BCE |
| Late (Post-urban) | ~1900–1300 BCE |

### 2.2 Major Sites
| Site | Location (Modern State/Country) | Unique Feature |
|------|---------------------------------|----------------|
| Mohenjo-daro | Sindh, Pakistan | Great Bath, Granary |
| Harappa | Punjab, Pakistan | First discovered (1921); two rows of six granaries |
| Lothal | Gujarat | Dockyard, bead factory, fire altars |
| Kalibangan | Rajasthan | Pre-Harappan ploughed field; fire altars |
| Dholavira | Gujarat | Largest IVC site in India; stepwell, stadium |
| Rakhigarhi | Haryana | Largest IVC site overall (recent surveys) |
| Banawali | Haryana | Lapis lazuli, barley cultivation |
| Surkotada | Gujarat | Horse bones (only site) |
| Chanhu-daro | Sindh | Inkpot, bead-making, no citadel |

### 2.3 Key Features
| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Town Planning | Grid pattern roads; underground drainage; two-part cities (citadel + lower town) |
| Script | Pictographic; undeciphered (~400 signs); Boustrophedon style |
| Trade | Long-distance (Mesopotamia — "Meluhha"); barter; standard weights (1:2:4:8:16:32:64) |
| Religion | Mother goddess; Proto-Shiva (Pashupati seal); trees (peepal); no temples found |
| Economy | Agriculture (wheat, barley, cotton — world's first); animal husbandry |
| Technology | Bronze; no iron; kiln-baked bricks (ratio 1:2:4) |
| Art | Bronze dancing girl; Priest-King (steatite); animal seals |

### 2.4 Decline — Theories
| Theory | Proponent/Evidence |
|--------|--------------------|
| Aryan Invasion | Wheeler (skeletons at Mohenjo-daro); contested |
| Flood/Climate change | Most accepted; drying of Saraswati (Ghaggar-Hakra) |
| Tectonic activity | Earthquakes, change in river courses |
| Epidemic | Less evidence |

> **✅ MUST REMEMBER:** IVC people did NOT know iron. Cotton was first cultivated here. Script = undeciphered.

## 3. Vedic Age

### 3.1 Early Vedic Period (~1500–1000 BCE)
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Text | Rigveda (1028 hymns, 10 mandalas) — oldest |
| Society | Tribal (Jana → Vis → Grama); Varna = occupation-based, NOT birth |
| Economy | Pastoral; cattle = wealth (Gavisti = war for cattle) |
| Polity | Raja (tribal chief), Sabha (elders), Samiti (general assembly) |
| Gods | Indra (most hymns), Agni (2nd), Varuna (cosmic order/Rita) |
| Women | Relatively free; Gargi, Lopamudra composed hymns |
| Geography | Sapta-Sindhu (seven rivers) |

### 3.2 Later Vedic Period (~1000–600 BCE)
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Texts | Samaveda, Yajurveda, Atharvaveda; Upanishads, Aranyakas, Brahmanas |
| Society | Varna became hereditary; Shudras added; Ashrama system |
| Economy | Agriculture primary; iron tools (Krishnaayasa); rice cultivation |
| Polity | Bigger kingdoms; Rajasuya/Ashvamedha/Vajapeya rituals |
| Religion | Brahma/Vishnu/Shiva rise; elaborate sacrifices (Yajnas) |
| Women | Status declined; child marriage, Sati mentioned |

### 3.3 Vedic Literature Summary
| Text | Type | Content |
|------|------|---------|
| Rigveda | Samhita | Hymns to gods |
| Samaveda | Samhita | Melodies (basis of Indian music) |
| Yajurveda | Samhita | Sacrificial formulae |
| Atharvaveda | Samhita | Spells, charms, medicine |
| Brahmanas | Prose | Ritual explanations |
| Aranyakas | Forest texts | Transition to philosophy |
| Upanishads | Philosophy | Brahman-Atman (108 Upanishads; 12 principal) |
| Vedangas | Auxiliary | 6 (Shiksha/Kalpa/Vyakaran/Nirukta/Chhandas/Jyotisha) |

> **⚠️ Trap:** Rigveda is oldest; Atharvaveda added LAST. Upanishads = end of Veda = Vedanta.

## 4. Buddhism

### 4.1 Siddhartha Gautama
| Event | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Birth | 563 BCE, Lumbini (Nepal) — Shakya clan |
| Enlightenment | 528 BCE, Bodh Gaya (Bihar), under peepal (Bodhi) tree |
| First Sermon | Sarnath (Deer Park) — "Dhammachakrapavattana" |
| Death (Mahaparinirvana) | 483 BCE, Kushinagar (UP) |

### 4.2 Four Noble Truths (Arya Satya)
1. **Dukkha** — Life is suffering
2. **Samudaya** — Desire is cause of suffering
3. **Nirodha** — Cessation of desire ends suffering
4. **Magga** — The path (Eightfold Path) leads to cessation

### 4.3 Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga)
| Category | Elements |
|----------|----------|
| **Wisdom (Prajna)** | Right View, Right Intention |
| **Morality (Sila)** | Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood |
| **Meditation (Samadhi)** | Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, Right Concentration |

### 4.4 Buddhist Councils
| Council | Date | Place | Presided by | Patronage | Outcome |
|---------|------|-------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| 1st | 483 BCE | Rajgriha | Mahakassapa | Ajatashatru | Compiled Vinaya & Dhamma |
| 2nd | 383 BCE | Vaishali | Sabakami | Kalashoka | Split: Sthavira vs Mahasanghika |
| 3rd | 250 BCE | Pataliputra | Moggaliputta-Tissa | Ashoka | Compiled Abhidhamma Pitaka; sent missionaries |
| 4th | 1st century CE | Kundalvana (Kashmir) | Vasumitra | Kanishka | Divided into Hinayana & Mahayana |

### 4.5 Sects Comparison
| Feature | Hinayana | Mahayana | Vajrayana |
|---------|----------|----------|-----------|
| Meaning | Lesser Vehicle | Greater Vehicle | Diamond/Thunderbolt Vehicle |
| Ideal | Arhat (individual salvation) | Bodhisattva (universal) | Tantric path |
| Language | Pali | Sanskrit | Sanskrit/Tibetan |
| Buddha | Human teacher | Divine/Godlike | Cosmic force |
| Spread | Sri Lanka, SE Asia | China, Japan, Korea | Tibet, Nepal |
| Also called | Theravada | — | Tantrayana |

### 4.6 Buddhist Texts
| Text | Content |
|------|---------|
| Vinaya Pitaka | Monastic rules |
| Sutta Pitaka | Buddha's teachings (dialogues) |
| Abhidhamma Pitaka | Philosophical analysis |
| Jataka tales | Previous lives of Buddha |
| Milindapanho | Dialogue: King Menander & Nagasena |

> **✅ MUST REMEMBER:** Pali was language of Hinayana. Bodh Gaya = Enlightenment; Sarnath = First Sermon; Kushinagar = Death; Lumbini = Birth.

## 5. Jainism

### 5.1 Mahavira
| Event | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Birth | 599 BCE, Vaishali (Bihar) — Kshatriya Lichchhavi clan |
| Parents | Siddhartha (father), Trishala (mother) |
| Enlightenment | 527 BCE (after 12 years of penance), Jrimbhikagrama |
| Nirvana | 527 BCE, Pawapuri (Bihar) |
| Title | Jina (conqueror), Mahavira, Tirthankara (24th and last) |

### 5.2 Triratna (Three Jewels)
1. **Samyak Darshana** — Right Faith
2. **Samyak Jnana** — Right Knowledge
3. **Samyak Charitra** — Right Conduct

### 5.3 Five Vows (Pancha Mahavrata)
| Vow | Meaning |
|-----|---------|
| Ahimsa | Non-violence |
| Satya | Truthfulness |
| Asteya | Non-stealing |
| Brahmacharya | Celibacy (added by Mahavira; earlier 4 by Parshvanatha) |
| Aparigraha | Non-possession |

### 5.4 Key Doctrines
| Doctrine | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Anekantavada | Many-sidedness of truth (no absolute truth) |
| Syadvada | Theory of conditional predication (maybe/perhaps) |
| Ahimsa | Extreme non-violence (even microorganisms) |
| Karma | Soul accumulates karma through actions |

### 5.5 Sects
| Sect | Clothes | Scripture Language | Key Difference |
|------|---------|-------------------|----------------|
| Digambara | Sky-clad (naked) | Prakrit | Women cannot attain moksha |
| Shvetambara | White-robed | Ardhamagadhi | Women can attain moksha |

> **⚠️ Trap:** 23rd Tirthankara = Parshvanatha (gave 4 vows). Mahavira added Brahmacharya (5th vow). Rishabhadeva = 1st Tirthankara.

## 6. Mauryan Empire (321–185 BCE)

### 6.1 Rulers
| Ruler | Reign | Key Events |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| Chandragupta Maurya | 321–297 BCE | Founded empire; defeated Nanda; treaty with Seleucus (305 BCE); converted to Jainism; fasted to death (Sravana Belgola) |
| Bindusara | 297–273 BCE | "Amitraghata" (slayer of enemies); conquered Deccan; Greek ambassador Deimachus |
| Ashoka | 273–232 BCE | Kalinga War (261 BCE) → converted to Buddhism; spread Dhamma; sent missionaries |

### 6.2 Administration
| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Capital | Pataliputra (modern Patna) |
| System | Centralized; provinces (Janapadas) under viceroys (princes) |
| Officials | Yuktas (revenue), Rajukas (judicial), Pradesikas (provincial) |
| Espionage | Elaborate spy system (Mahamattas) |
| Army | Vast 4-fold: elephant, chariot, cavalry, infantry |

### 6.3 Ashoka's Edicts
| Type | Content |
|------|---------|
| Rock Edicts (14 Major) | Dhamma principles; welfare measures; Kalinga regret |
| Minor Rock Edicts | Personal declarations; conversion to Buddhism |
| Pillar Edicts (7 Major) | Administrative orders; Dhamma Mahamattas |
| Minor Pillar Edicts | Specific instructions |

**Locations of important Edicts:**
- Kalinga Edicts: Dhauli (Odisha) & Jaugada
- Kandahar Edict: Bilingual (Greek + Aramaic)
- Rampurva/Lauriya Nandangarh: Lion Pillars

### 6.4 Dhamma Principles
- Respect for elders, parents, teachers
- Ahimsa (no animal slaughter for sacrifices)
- Religious tolerance
- Philanthropy (wells, rest houses, hospitals)

### 6.5 Arthashastra
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Author | Kautilya (also Chanakya/Vishnugupta) |
| Content | 15 books; statecraft, economy, military, spies, diplomacy |
| Saptanga Theory | 7 elements of state: Swami/Amatya/Janapada/Durga/Kosha/Danda/Mitra |
| Mandal Theory | Neighbor is enemy; neighbor's neighbor is friend |

> **✅ MUST REMEMBER:** Lion Capital (Sarnath) = National Emblem. Ashoka Chakra = National Flag. Arthashastra rediscovered by R. Shamasastry (1904).

## 8. Gupta Empire (319–550 CE)

### 8.1 Rulers
| Ruler | Reign | Achievement |
|-------|-------|------------|
| Chandragupta I | 319–335 CE | Founded empire; Gupta Era (320 CE); married Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi |

| Kumaragupta I | 415–455 CE | Founded Nalanda University |
| Skandagupta | 455–467 CE | Repelled Hunas; last great Gupta king |

### 8.2 Golden Age Achievements
| Field | Achievement |
|-------|------------|
| Science | Aryabhata (zero, pi=3.14, Earth rotates, solar year); Varahamihira (Pancha Siddhantika); Brahmagupta |
| Literature | Kalidasa (Abhijnanasakuntalam, Meghaduta, Kumarasambhava, Raghuvamsha, Ritusamhara, Malavikagnimitra); Vishakhadatta (Mudrarakshasa) |

| Art | Ajanta caves (early frescoes); Deogarh Temple; Dashavatara Temple |
| Religion | Revival of Hinduism; Bhagavatism; Vaishnavism dominant |
| Administration | Decentralized; liberal; land grants to Brahmins |

### 8.3 Fa Hien's Account (399–414 CE)
- Visited during Chandragupta II's reign
- People prosperous, no death penalty, charity common
- Buddhism flourishing

> **⚠️ Trap:** Aryabhata → 5th century CE (Gupta period), NOT ancient. He did NOT invent zero as a numeral; he used place value system. Zero as symbol was later.

| Slave/Ilbari | 1206–1290 | Qutb-ud-din Aibak, Iltutmish, Razia Sultana, Balban | Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque; first woman ruler (Razia); Theory of Divine Right (Balban) |

## 12. Mughal Empire (1526–1857)

### 12.1 Rulers Timeline
| Ruler | Reign | Key Battles | Key Contributions |
|-------|-------|------------|-------------------|
| Babur | 1526–1530 | Panipat I (1526), Khanwa (1527), Ghaghra (1529) | Founded Mughal dynasty; introduced gunpowder; wrote Tuzuk-i-Baburi (Baburnama) |
| Humayun | 1530–1540, 1555–1556 | Battle of Chausa (1539, lost to Sher Shah); recaptured 1555 | Sher Shah Sur intervened (1540–1555); Humayun lost/regained throne |
| Akbar | 1556–1605 | Panipat II (1556), Haldighati (1576) | Din-i-Ilahi, Sulh-i-Kul; Mansabdari; abolished Jizya; Navratnas; Fatehpur Sikri |
| Jahangir | 1605–1627 | — | Nurjahan's influence; Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri; Paintings (European influence) |
| Shah Jahan | 1627–1658 | — | Taj Mahal; Red Fort; Peacock Throne; "Shah-i-Buland Iqbal" |
| Aurangzeb | 1658–1707 | — | Reimposed Jizya; Deccan campaigns; Shivaji's escape; most extensive empire; religious intolerance; wrote Fatawa-e-Alamgiri |

### 12.2 Mansabdari System
- Mansab = rank (Zat = personal; Sawar = cavalry)
- 33 grades (10 to 10,000)
- Mansabdars paid by jagirs (land grants)

### 12.3 Mughal Administration
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Central | Emperor → Wakil (PM) → Diwan (Finance) → Mir Bakshi (Military) → Sadr-us-Sudur (Religious) |
| Provincial | Subedar → Diwan → Faujdar → Kotwal |
| Revenue | Todar Mal's Zabt system (measurement of land); Dahsala (10-year assessment) |
| Army | Mansabdari, cavalry-dominant |

### 12.4 Mughal Art & Architecture
| Ruler | Architecture/Art |
|-------|-----------------|
| Akbar | Fatehpur Sikri; Agra Fort; Humayun's Tomb; blended Hindu-Islamic |
| Jahangir | Portraits; Tuzuk paintings; Shalimar Bagh (Kashmir) |
| Shah Jahan | Taj Mahal; Red Fort; Jama Masjid (Delhi); Moti Masjid (Agra) |
| Aurangzeb | Bibi ka Maqbara (Aurangabad); Badshahi Mosque (Lahore) |

> **✅ MUST REMEMBER:** Akbar's Navratnas: Todar Mal, Birbal, Abul Fazl, Faizi, Tansen, Man Singh, Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khana, Fakir Aziao-Din, Mullah Do Piaza.

### 15.3 Marathas
| Ruler/Period | Key Facts |
