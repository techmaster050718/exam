## 2. Tri-service and joint exercises

- Joint military exercises improve interoperability across army, navy, and air force.
- Tri-service drills often focus on joint planning, logistics, and command coordination.
- Exercises also build responses to humanitarian crises, maritime threats, and regional security challenges.

---

## 3. Army exercises

- Yudh Abhyas, Indra, Dharma Guardian, and similar bilateral army drills are important exam references.
- These exercises test mechanised operations, tactics, and planning under realistic conditions.

---

## 4. Navy exercises

- Varuna, Malabar, AUSINDEX, and similar maritime exercises strengthen naval interoperability.
- They are especially relevant in the Indo-Pacific strategic context.

---

## 5. Air force exercises

- Cope India and Garuda Shakti are examples of significant bilateral air exercises.
- Air exercises improve fighter pilot coordination, tactical manoeuvre, and logistics support.

---

## 6. Quick revision one-liners

- Malabar is a major Indo-Pacific naval exercise.
- Yudh Abhyas is a major India–USA army exercise.
- Cope India highlights India–USA air-force cooperation.
- Varuna reflects India–France naval training cooperation.
- Tiger Triumph combines joint humanitarian and disaster-response drills.

---

## 7. Common exam traps

- Exercise participants and frequency can change; use current public sources for volatile details.
- Do not infer a permanent alliance from a single exercise.
- Distinguish bilateral training from multilateral strategic forums.

---

## 8. Source and verification

Use official ministry, service headquarters, and public defence partnership announcements. This module keeps to high-level, exam-relevant public facts rather than sensitive participation or operational detail.
