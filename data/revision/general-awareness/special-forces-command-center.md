### Broad exam points
- Para SF is the army’s principal special operations unit.
- Marcos are the Indian Navy’s elite maritime special operations force.
- Garud is tasked with air force special operations and airfield protection.
- SFF has a special strategic role with historical connection to India’s security architecture.
- NSG is not a conventional military force; it is a specialised national security unit with counter-terror responsibilities.

### Core distinction: special ops vs regular combat
- Regular units are structured for broad-spectrum battlefield operations.
- Special operations units are optimised for speed, secrecy, infiltration, precision strikes, and high-risk missions.
- In exams, the main differentiator is mission profile, parent service, and operating domain rather than the generic label “special force.”

## 2. Para SF

- Parent organisation: Indian Army.
- Role: airborne, strike, reconnaissance, and unconventional warfare tasks.
- Training: intense selection, special operations training, high-altitude competence, and tactical endurance.
- Capabilities: infiltration, surveillance, raiding, mountain warfare, and quick reaction operations.
- Publicly known role: strategic and tactical special operations in hostile and difficult terrain.

### Exam relevance
- Frequently asked in defence and CAPF sections as the leading Indian special operations unit.
- Special operations are distinct from conventional infantry action.

---

## 3. MARCOS

- Parent organisation: Indian Navy.
- Role: maritime special operations, combat swimming, boarding, reconnaissance, and rescue tasks.
- Domain: sea, coast, riverine, and littoral operations.
- Training: specialised maritime, diving, and tactical fitness training.
- Notable public role: high-readiness intervention force for maritime threats and rescue scenarios.

---

## 4. Garud Commandos

- Parent organisation: Indian Air Force.
- Role: protecting air assets, countering threats to air bases, and conducting special operations support.
- Operating domain: airfields, high-value assets, and airborne insertion support.
- Training: air assault, tactical mobility, and specialised security operations.

---

## 5. Special Frontier Force (SFF)

- Parent/administrative context: strategic special operations force with a distinct historical origin.
- Role: high-altitude reconnaissance, special operations, and sensitive strategic missions.
- Domain: difficult mountain and border terrain.
- Broad public understanding: not a general-purpose combat formation, but a specialised strategic force outside normal army command structures in public narrative.

---

## 6. NSG and high-risk security response

- NSG is a specialised counter-terror and anti-hijack response force.
- Primary responsibilities include hostage rescue and critical infrastructure protection.
- It complements regular police and military agencies in security emergencies.

### Comparison
| Force | Parent | Typical domain | Typical task |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Para SF | Army | Mountain / deep operations | Tactical special operations |
| Marcos | Navy | Sea / coast | Maritime intervention |
| Garud | Air Force | Air bases / air operations | Airfield security and support |
| NSG | MHA | Urban / high-risk sites | Counter-terror response |

### Force-specific memory lines
- Para SF: deep-penetration, covert insertion, surveillance, mountain and border-sensitive operations.
- MARCOS: maritime raids, intercept, coastal security support, and high-risk rescue/intervention.
- Garud: airfield security, force protection, and special operations air support.
- SFF: strategic high-altitude and intelligence-oriented operational role.
- NSG: anti-terror, anti-hijack, hostages, VVIP security support and critical-site response.

### Doctrinal exam angle
- Special forces are not a single monolithic category: their role depends on parent service and mission profile.
- Military special forces focus on warfighting and tactical strike/recce roles.
- Central specialised agencies like NSG focus on internal security and crisis response.
- In exams, the tendency is to ask the “parent service + primary role + domain” triad, not technical unit-level operational secrets.

## 8. Common exam traps

- Do not confuse NSG with regular police or military formations.
- Do not treat special forces as a generic term for all elite units.
- Para SF, Marcos, and Garud are separate service-specific formations with different parent organisations.
