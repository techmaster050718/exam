### Organisational hierarchy and service links
- Ministry of Defence is the political and administrative apex.
- Chief of Defence Staff is the principal military adviser for jointness; details can vary by government and organisational structure over time.
- Service headquarters are separate command chains for Army, Navy, and Air Force.
- Training institutions such as NDA, IMA, Air Force Academy, and Naval Academy create the officer pipeline.
- Research and production organisations sustain platform and system science.

### Coast Guard
- Role: maritime security, coastal surveillance, search and rescue, anti-smuggling and anti-poaching enforcement.
- Headquarters: New Delhi.
- Service context: distinct from the Navy, though closely linked in coastal security and maritime domain awareness.
- Exam fact: Coast Guard is a maritime security agency with a public-service and law-enforcement dimension, not a combat branch in the same sense as the Navy.

## 6. Special Forces and elite units

### PARA SF
- Parent service: Indian Army
- Role: special operations, reconnaissance, direct action
- Operating environment: mountain, desert, urban, covert operations support
- Exam fact: Para SF is not the same as Rashtriya Rifles or Ghatak

### Ghatak
- Parent service: Indian Army
- Role: assault and close combat specialist infantry role
- Exam fact: Ghatak is a force multiplier and a specialist assault capability, not a para-drop special operations unit in the same sense as Para SF

### Rashtriya Rifles (RR)
- Parent service: Indian Army
- Role: counter-insurgency and counter-terrorism operations
- Exam fact: distinct from Para SF, which is a special operations force

### MARCOS
- Parent service: Indian Navy
- Role: maritime special operations and intervention
- Exam fact: maritime special operations, not a land force

### Garud
- Parent service: Indian Air Force
- Role: airbase security and special operations support
- Exam fact: AIF special force connected with air force security role

### SFF (Special Frontier Force)
- Parent service: strategic special operations tradition under specific institutional context
- Role: special mission and strategic support tasks
- Exam fact: distinct from regular Army and para special forces

### NSG / SAG
- Parent service: central security apparatus and internal security structure
- Role: counter-terrorism response, hostage-rescue, elite intervention
- Exam fact: distinct from military special forces

### AFSOD
- AFSOD = Armed Forces Special Operations Division
- Role: special operations integration and strategic coordination
- Exam fact: important in modern defence structure but details remain public-policy/official-institution-oriented and should not be over-specified

### Para SF vs MARCOS vs Garud
| Unit | Parent service | Broad role | Operating environment | Publicly known functions | Exam fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Para SF | Army | Special operations | Mountain, desert, urban, covert | Reconnaissance, direct action, special tasks | Elite Army special force |
| MARCOS | Navy | Maritime special ops | Sea, littoral, coastal areas | Maritime intervention, reconnaissance | Maritime special force |
| Garud | Air Force | Airbase security / special ops support | Airfield, airborne support | Security and special service support | IAF elite force |

### RR vs Para SF
| Factor | Rashtriya Rifles | Para SF |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Parent structure | Army | Army |
| Main role | Counter-insurgency | Special operations |
| Domain | Internal security and anti-terror context | Special missions and reconnaissance |
| Exam fact | Counter-insurgency force | Elite special operations unit |

### Ghatak vs Para SF
| Factor | Ghatak | Para SF |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Role | Assault and close combat specialist infantry role | Special operations |
| Orientation | Combat support and assault | Special missions and deep ops |
| Exam fact | Distinct from para special force | Distinct from Ghatak |

### Army SF vs NSG
| Factor | Army SF | NSG |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Parent organization | Army | Central security apparatus |
| Main role | Special operations | Counter-terror response |
| Exam fact | Military special operations | Elite anti-terror force |

## 9. Major military operations and public facts

### Operation Vijay (Goa, 1961)
- Year: 1961
- Theatre: Goa
- Force: Army, Navy, Air Force
- Objective: End Portuguese rule in Goa
- Outcome: Goa integrated into India

### Operation Meghdoot (1984)
- Year: 1984
- Theatre: Siachen
- Force: Indian Army
- Objective: Occupy key heights in Siachen
- Outcome: India secured critical positions and strengthened high-altitude presence

### Operation Pawan (1987–90)
- Year: 1987–90
- Theatre: Sri Lanka
- Force: Indian Army (IPKF)
- Objective: Support peace process and manage security issues

### Operation Cactus (1988)
- Year: 1988
- Theatre: Maldives
- Force: Armed Forces
- Objective: Support political stability and intervention at the request of the Maldivian government

### Operation Vijay (Kargil, 1999)
- Year: 1999
- Theatre: Kargil sector
- Force: Army, Air Force
- Objective: Evict intruders from Kargil positions
- Outcome: Major high-altitude war and strategic demonstration

### 26/11 Mumbai attacks
- Year: 2008
- Theatre: Mumbai
- Force: NSG, police, security agencies
- Objective: Respond to coordinated attacks
- Outcome: security reforms and a strong emphasis on coastal security

### Surgical strikes (2016)
- Year: 2016
- Theatre: LoC context
- Force: Army
- Objective: Retaliatory cross-border action after terrorist attacks

### Balakot (2019)
- Year: 2019
- Theatre: Pakistan airspace context
- Force: IAF
- Objective: Retaliatory strike after Pulwama attack

### LAC developments (2020 onward)
- Year: 2020 onward
- Theatre: Ladakh / LAC
- Force: Army, Air Force, other forces
- Objective: Border security and high-altitude readiness

## 10. Exam-oriented quick revision: 10-second facts

- LA: Army, Navy, Air Force are separate services.
- Commands and theatres are key exam facts.
- NSG is not the same as NSG in nuclear non-proliferation context.
- Para SF, MARCOS, Garud, Ghatak and RR are distinct units.
- BrahMos, Agni, Akash, Astra, Nag are major missile names.
- Malabar, Varuna, Yudh Abhyas, Shakti, Milan are common exercise names.

## 11. Common traps

- Para SF vs RR vs Ghatak.
- NSG vs Nuclear Suppliers Group.
- Operation Vijay 1961 vs Operation Vijay 1999.
- Military intelligence vs intelligence bureau vs RAW.

## 12. Source and verification

Use official public statements, Ministry of Defence publications, PIB, service officers’ historical accounts, and credible public military histories. Operational details and sensitive tactical procedures must remain limited to officially confirmed public facts only.
