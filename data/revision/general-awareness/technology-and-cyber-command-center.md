### Core concepts
- Malware: malicious software designed to damage or disrupt systems.
- Ransomware: malware that encrypts data and demands payment.
- Phishing: deceptive digital communication to steal credentials or data.
- DDoS: distributed denial of service; floods a service with traffic.
- Zero-day: vulnerability not yet publicly patched.
- Botnets: networks of compromised devices used for attacks.
- APT: advanced persistent threat.
- FBI / CERT-In / NCIIPC references are examples of public institutional frameworks in India’s cyber domain, but the precise mandate and institutional details should be checked against official sources.

### Security controls
- Firewall, IDS, IPS, antivirus, endpoint protection, MFA, PKI, digital signatures, SIEM, SOC.
- Public-key infrastructure ensures secure trust for digital signatures and identity systems.

## 2. AI, deepfakes and national security

- Generative AI uses large models for text, image, and code generation.
- LLMs are large language models.
- Deepfakes can manipulate audio and visual content and create social or security risks.
- AI security includes securing AI models, data, and deployment pipelines.
- AI warfare and AI-based surveillance are strategic and policy concerns, not necessarily public military doctrine in full detail.

### Exam link

## 5. Nuclear security and strategic doctrine

### India’s strategic framework
- India maintains a nuclear doctrine shaped by deterrence and national security considerations.
- The Nuclear Command Authority is a public institutional concept tied to strategic command and control.
- Nuclear triad refers to land, air, and sea-based nuclear delivery capability.
- Strategic Forces Command is connected with operational custody and deployment of nuclear assets.

### Civilian nuclear programme and institutions
- Department of Atomic Energy (DAE)
- Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC)
- Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited (NPCIL)
- Heavy Water Board and associated fuel cycle institutions
- Reactors and domestic power generation programmes remain important in strategic and energy security contexts

### Related terms
- No First Use (NFU)
- Strategic Forces Command
- Ballistic missile submarines
- NSG vs National Security Guard

### International context
- NSG: Nuclear Suppliers Group in export-control and non-proliferation context
- IAEA: International Atomic Energy Agency
- NPT/CTBT/MTCR: relevant in non-proliferation and strategic control debates, but should be treated as policy and legal context rather than operational fact

### Important trap
- NSG means National Security Guard in Indian internal security context.
- NSG also means Nuclear Suppliers Group in nuclear non-proliferation and export control contexts.
