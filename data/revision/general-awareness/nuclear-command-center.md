## 1. Nuclear basics relevant for exams

### Nuclear command map
| Domain | Publicly known meaning | Exam use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Civilian nuclear energy | Power generation and scientific research | Energy and science policy |
| Strategic nuclear doctrine | Deterrence, command and control, triad | Defence and geopolitics |
| Non-proliferation | Safeguards, export control, treaty architecture | International relations |
| Nuclear safety | Regulation, oversight, radiation protection | Governance and public policy |

### Key concepts
- Fission: splitting of a heavy nucleus into smaller nuclei, releasing energy.
- Fusion: combining light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing large energy under suitable conditions.
- Critical mass: minimum mass required to sustain a chain reaction.
- Nuclear fuel cycle: mining, enrichment, fuel fabrication, reactor operation, spent fuel management.
- Moderator: slows neutrons to sustain a controlled chain reaction.
- Control rods: absorb neutrons and regulate reactor output.

### Why it matters
- Nuclear energy, nuclear safety, and strategic deterrence are repeated in defence and science-based GK sections.

## 2. Indian institutions

| Institution | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| DAE | Department of Atomic Energy |
| BARC | Bhabha Atomic Research Centre |
| NPCIL | Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited |
| AERB | Atomic Energy Regulatory Board |
| IAEA | International Atomic Energy Agency |

### Publicly relevant points
- DAE oversees India’s atomic energy programme.
- BARC is a leading research and technology institution in nuclear science.
- NPCIL manages civilian nuclear power generation.
- AERB regulates nuclear safety and licensing in the civilian domain.

---

## 3. Civilian nuclear power

- India uses nuclear power as a low-carbon energy source.
- Nuclear plants are built under strict safety oversight.
- Reactor types, fuel management, heavy water, and waste management are important in exam contexts.
- Civilian nuclear power is distinct from strategic nuclear doctrine.

---

## 4. Strategic nuclear context

- India’s nuclear doctrine is commonly discussed in terms of deterrence and minimum credible deterrence.
- The concept of a nuclear triad refers to land, air, and sea-based nuclear delivery vectors.
- Strategic Forces Command is associated with operational control of nuclear assets in public discourse.
- No operational details or sensitive deployment information should be added in broad exam notes.

### Core terms
- No First Use (NFU)
- Deterrence
- Triad
- Strategic Forces Command
- Command and control

## 5. International non-proliferation context

| Organisation / agreement | Public context |
| :--- | :--- |
| NPT | Non-Proliferation Treaty |
| CTBT | Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty |
| NSG | Nuclear Suppliers Group |
| IAEA | International atomic energy safeguards and monitoring |

### Exam attention
- NSG can mean National Security Guard in domestic security contexts and Nuclear Suppliers Group in nuclear diplomacy.
- In nuclear context, NSG refers to non-proliferation export controls.

---

## 6. India’s nuclear position

- India is not a signatory to NPT, though it has played a significant role in nuclear non-proliferation debates.
- India’s nuclear policy is shaped by security, deterrence, and technological self-reliance.
- Energy security and strategic security are treated as distinct but connected themes.

## 8. Common exam traps

- Nuclear power and nuclear weapons are not the same concept.
- NSG and NSG are context-dependent acronyms.
- Civilian institutions must not be confused with strategic command structures.
- Public exams should avoid classified operational details.
