## 3. India–China

### Strategic lens
- LAC: Line of Actual Control.
- India and China have long-standing border and infrastructure issues.
- Major areas of concern include the western sector, Eastern Ladakh, Pangong Tso, Galwan, Depsang, and broader border infrastructure.
- Strategic relevance includes Himalayan geography, trade, technology, and security competition.

### Keywords
- LAC, Galwan, Pangong Tso, Doklam, Aksai Chin, Arunachal Pradesh, border infrastructure.

---

### Strategic lens
- LoC: Line of Control.
- International Border: the land boundary across multiple states.
- Kashmir remains a core issue in bilateral relations.
- Indus Waters Treaty remains a vital legal and strategic issue.
- Sir Creek, Siachen, terrorism, and cross-border security remain recurring flashpoints.

### Keywords
- LoC, Kashmir, Indus Waters, Sir Creek, Siachen, cross-border security.

## 6. Indo-Pacific and strategic frameworks

### QUAD
- India, US, Japan, Australia.
- Focus on maritime security, supply chains, tech, and infrastructure cooperation.

### BRICS
- Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa.
- Platform for emerging economies and geopolitical coordination.

### SCO
- Shanghai Cooperation Organisation.
- Focuses on security, regional cooperation, and political dialogue.

### BIMSTEC
- Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation.
- Important for connectivity and regionalisation.

## 7. Maritime security and chokepoints

- Strait of Hormuz: energy route.
- Bab-el-Mandeb: Red Sea route.
- Malacca Strait: key maritime chokepoint.
- Persian Gulf: energy corridor.
- Indian Ocean: critical for trade and strategic access.
- Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep are strategically important maritime nodes.

### Why it matters
- Maritime security impacts energy supplies, trade, naval strategy, and political geography.

## 10. Quick revision one-liners
